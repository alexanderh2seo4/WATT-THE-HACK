# Electricity interruption analysis

Analyzed **2,199,294 reported records**, 2012–2024, from 13 annual workbooks.
**839,787 records (38.18%) are unplanned**.
Percentages pool records across years; they are not averages of yearly percentages.

The detailed analysis below distinguishes typical duration, extreme reported duration, long-outage probability,
and customer/capacity burden. All findings are regenerated by the script.

## Detailed findings: what lasts longest and what causes most impact?

### Findings at a glance

- **Longest typical outages:** Force majeure, with a median of **166 minutes**, a 90th percentile of **23.98 hours**, and a mean of **12.50 hours**. This comparison uses unplanned cause categories with at least 100 records.
- **Longest individual reported unplanned interruption:** **527,583 minutes (366.38 days)**, classified as **Force majeure**, starting 2021-07-15 02:30:00. Source: [2021, row 6759](../incident_records_2021.xlsx), worksheet `VUS 2021`. This extreme source value is unverified; it is not evidence that every affected customer was continuously disconnected for that time.
- **Day-long outages:** **4,828 unplanned records (0.57% of records with a valid duration)** exceed 24 hours. Together they account for **14.08% of reported unplanned LV customer-minutes** and **13.39% of unplanned MV network MVA-minutes**.
- **Largest total LV customer burden:** Operator responsibility / unknown cause, accounting for **57.91%** of reported unplanned LV customer-minutes. Frequency and severity answer different questions.
- **Impact is concentrated:** the top **1% of LV records with observed customer-minute impact** account for **28.00%** of that reported unplanned burden.

The files identify broad **reported causes**, not specific technical defects. They cannot tell us whether a long interruption was caused by a failed insulator, a fallen tree, a broken conductor, or a particular storm. All conclusions below are descriptive associations within these records.

#### Impact consistency checks and sensitivity

Some reported impact values need review as well as the very long durations. For paired observations, this diagnostic flags an integral **greater than reported affected size × full duration**, allowing a relative tolerance of one millionth (and an absolute tolerance of one millionth unit-minute). Staged restoration can explain a smaller integral; it does not by itself explain a larger value. Source aggregation, inconsistent fields or reporting errors may require investigation; no values are corrected here.

### 10. Implications for the hackathon scenario

- Use **median and upper percentiles by cause and voltage** when choosing illustrative restoration-duration scenarios; a single source maximum is a poor default.
- Report **frequency and burden separately**. A common operator/unknown category and a less frequent severe-event category may warrant different demonstration priorities.
- Treat weather and force-majeure categories as broad historical context. The data do not identify which mapped Bavarian line is damaged, which line is live, where customers are connected, or which defect a sensor would observe.
- Any use of these frequencies as simulated fault priors, or any claimed customer/financial benefit from drone deployment, requires additional explicitly stated assumptions. The dataset cannot measure drone effectiveness or causally attribute faster restoration to an inspection strategy.

## Interpretation and limitations

- A row is a **reported interruption record**, not necessarily one physical fault. Meter replacements can be annual bulk reports; connected networks may report effects of the same upstream fault. No records are deduplicated on coincident timestamps.
- Cause labels are reporting categories. Operator responsibility also includes unknown causes. Atmospheric effects include multiple weather phenomena; force majeure may also include extreme weather. Neither category is a storm-only count or a fault probability for the hackathon map.
- Use reported impact integrals directly: staged restoration means customer count × full duration can differ from reported customer-minutes. Customer-interruption totals count repeated interruptions, not unique people or households. MV MVA-minutes measure interrupted transformer capacity over time, not lost energy or financial damage.
- LV = customer counts/minutes; MV = MVA/MVA-minutes; HV/EHV = MW/MW-minutes. Network and end-user transformer metrics are separate. Ambiguous combinations of voltage levels remain in record/duration statistics but receive no inferred impact units.
- These are nationally reported long interruptions, generally over three minutes. Source rows at/below three minutes remain visible and are counted in the quality report. Short interruptions, exact customer locations, asset failure mechanisms and economic losses are unavailable.
- Official SAIDI EnWG uses selected unplanned causes and excludes force majeure and planned work. The script preserves the published index instead of dividing all reported impacts by national customer counts. Published figures are rounded and may not reconcile exactly with record totals or the displayed LV + MV sum.
- State indicators approximate geography using the network operator's registered office. They are not precise outage-location statistics and should not be averaged equally to reconstruct the national index.
- Incident years before 2012 are missing. National indicators cover 2006–2024; state indicators cover 2008–2024. The current PDF takes precedence over its archived workbook; overlapping values are checked rather than double-counted.
- File years take precedence over worksheet titles; timestamp disagreements are counted. Missing/negative numeric values are excluded from their metric only; source records remain in count denominators. No missing impacts are reconstructed.
- Cause labels are translated with case-insensitive matching; the source's unselected dropdown placeholder is labeled unknown/missing. Original labels are retained in the source files and top-impact record extract.

## Reproduce

```sh
python3 -m pip install -r scripts/requirements-analysis.txt
python3 scripts/analyze_bundesnetzagentur.py
```

Optional: `--input-dir PATH --output-dir PATH`.
Outputs include cause, planned status, voltage, month, duration band, cause-by-voltage and annual summaries;
all contain separate all-record and unplanned populations. `period=ALL` pools the available incident years.
`top_lv_customer_impact.csv` provides the top 20 unplanned LV records per year with source row references.
All `_pct` fields use 0–100 percentage points, not 0–1 fractions.

Additional CSVs include `cause_severity`, `duration_sensitivity`, `long_duration_thresholds`,
`longest_unplanned_records` (top 50), `longest_all_records`, `longest_unplanned_by_cause`,
`longest_unplanned_latest_year`, `impact_by_cause_within_voltage`, `impact_concentration`,
`highest_lv_customer_impact`, `highest_mv_network_impact`, `daily_unplanned_clusters`,
`monthly_unplanned_causes`, `unplanned_annual_trends`, and `state_indicator_changes`.
`impact_consistency_checks`, `impact_consistency_sensitivity` and `impact_records_for_review`
document source values whose reported integrals exceed the simple size-times-duration bound.
Row references use the original Excel row numbering, including the header row.

Sources: local workbooks/PDF listed in `../README.md`; [BNetzA indicator definitions](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Fachthemen/ElektrizitaetundGas/Versorgungssicherheit/Versorgungsunterbrechungen/Auswertung_Strom/artikel.html);
